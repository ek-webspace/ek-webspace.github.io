01 / PROFILE

Psychology as a foundation. Cognitive science as a bridge. Human–AI research as a direction.

My academic direction connects an understanding of human behavior with quantitative research and the study of intelligent systems.

My academic background began in psychology, where I developed an interest in human behavior, cognition, and the processes involved in judgment and decision-making.

I am currently pursuing a master's degree in Cognitive Science at the University of Messina. This interdisciplinary environment allows me to connect psychological theory with technology, philosophy, language, experimentation, and computational methods.

My developing research direction focuses on understanding what happens when people reason, decide, and act with artificial intelligence. The long-term objective is to contribute to Human–AI systems that support human judgment without encouraging inappropriate reliance or replacing meaningful human agency.

02 / RESEARCH

Studying people, decisions, and intelligent systems.

My interests concentrate on the behavioral consequences of interacting with AI and the design of systems that support better human reasoning rather than merely automate it.

RESEARCH AREA / 01

Human–AI Interaction

How people understand, evaluate, trust, question, and collaborate with artificial intelligence across different tasks and decision environments.

RESEARCH AREA / 02

Judgment and Decision-Making

How AI-generated information and recommendations affect choices, confidence, uncertainty, cognitive biases, and reliance on automated advice.

METHOD / 03

Behavioral Experimentation

Designing quantitative studies that produce interpretable evidence about human responses to intelligent systems.

PROBLEM / 04

Evaluation of AI Advice

Understanding how people assess the quality, credibility, uncertainty, and usefulness of AI-generated outputs.

DESIGN / 05

Human-Centered AI Design

Exploring how explanations, interfaces, and interaction structures influence decisions and preserve human agency.

06 / PRINCIPLES

The kind of research I aim to build.

These principles represent the standards I want future work to follow as my research experience develops.

01

Human-centered

Technology should be evaluated through its consequences for human understanding, agency, performance, and well-being.

02

Evidence-driven

Claims about Human–AI interaction should be supported by transparent methods, appropriate measurements, and interpretable evidence.

03

Reproducible

Research materials, analytical decisions, and computational workflows should be organized so that findings can be examined and reproduced.
